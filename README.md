# 📡 BlueHound Guard

Always-on lookout for Bluetooth trackers, for Android. The full-strength native
companion to [BlueHound](https://tmnesmn-bit.github.io/bluehound/) (the web version).

**What it does:** keeps scanning for Bluetooth devices even with your screen off,
shows everything nearby in plain English (🎧 Headphones · Sony, not raw numbers),
and sends you a notification if a known tracker type (AirTag / Find My, Tile,
Samsung SmartTag, Chipolo) stays near you for more than 15 minutes.

**What stays private:** everything. No account, no server, nothing leaves your phone.

## Install (2 minutes)

1. On your Android phone, open **[the latest release](../../releases/latest)** and tap **BlueHoundGuard.apk**.
2. When the download finishes, tap the file. Android will warn about installing
   apps from outside the Play Store — allow it for your browser when asked.
3. Open BlueHound Guard, tap **Start guard**, and allow Bluetooth (called
   "Nearby devices"), Location, and Notifications when your phone asks.
   Android requires location permission for Bluetooth scanning — the app never
   records or sends your location anywhere.
4. That's it. A small "BlueHound Guard is watching" notification means it's on.

## Honest limits

- **A quiet list is not a guarantee.** Trackers broadcast on and off, some go
  quiet when their owner is nearby, and trackers rotate their radio address —
  a tracker that rotates its address mid-ride can restart the 15-minute clock.
  If you feel unsafe, also use your phone's built-in unknown-tracker alerts,
  and trust your instincts.
- **Your own gear can be flagged.** Your own AirPods away from your phone, or a
  family member's tracker in the same car, will look like a "possible tracker."
  That's normal.
- **Battery:** low-power scan mode; expect a small but real battery cost while
  the guard is on.

## If you find a tracker that isn't yours

If you're worried about your safety: **don't destroy it** — it can be evidence —
and contact local police.

## Fair use

BlueHound Guard is for finding **your own** devices and for checking that no
unknown tracker is following you. Using any tool to track a person without their
consent is illegal in most places, including Minnesota.
